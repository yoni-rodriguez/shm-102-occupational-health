Course Description

Explore the fundamental concepts of occupational health, including identification of health hazards in the workplace, prevention of workplace injuries and illnesses, human factors, and environmental health as it relates to the workplace. Prerequisites: none.

SHM 102 uses real cases to examine how workers, tasks, hazards, exposures, health effects, risk, and controls connect across workplaces, communities, policy, the environment, and biology.

Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

  1. Explain occupational health professions and how they affect worker health.
  2. Address significant occupational and environmental health issues and articulate their broader impacts.
  3. Describe biological, chemical, ergonomic, physical, and psychosocial risk factors and hazards.
  4. Assess the importance of prevention and proactive behavior in reducing risk factors and hazards.
  5. Research occupational and environmental risk factors, hazards, exposures, and health effects for a selected work environment or problem.
  6. Investigate how human factors can influence workplace health.
  7. Prioritize recommendations using evidence and quantitative reasoning.
  8. Apply the course reasoning framework: Worker → Task → Hazard → Exposure → Health Effect → Risk → Controls.

Course Materials and Technology

There is no required commercial textbook for the course. Readings, case materials, government resources, research articles, and other materials will be provided or linked through Canvas. Some copyrighted readings may be available only through Canvas or CWU library access.

Students need regular access to Canvas, their CWU email, and a device capable of viewing course materials and submitting work. Poll Everywhere may be used for in class activities.

According to CWU guidance, one credit represents a total time commitment of approximately three hours each week, including scheduled class time and work outside class. A five credit course therefore represents a substantial weekly time commitment.

Course Organization and Approach

Rather than treating occupational hazards as isolated categories, the course examines worker health across multiple interconnected scales:

Climate & Environment
       ↓
Society & Policy
       ↓
Community Health
       ↓
Workplace Environment
       ↓
Worker Health & Behaviors
       ↓
Cells, Molecules & Microorganisms

Biological, chemical, ergonomic, physical, and psychosocial hazards are examined across these scales through real world occupational health cases.

Cases repeatedly use a common occupational health reasoning framework:

Worker → Task → Hazard → Exposure → Health Effect → Risk → Controls

This framework provides students with a consistent approach for examining occupational health problems throughout the course.

SHM 102 emphasizes:

  • Real world occupational and environmental health case studies
  • Connections between global, national, state, and local worker health issues
  • Scientific reasoning and evidence based decision making
  • Exposure pathways and health effects
  • Prevention through the hierarchy of controls
  • Interdisciplinary approaches to worker health
  • Current occupational and environmental health events
  • Active learning and application of course concepts

The course begins with foundations and then increasingly uses cases, discussion, evidence, and application.

Class meetings may combine short lectures, case studies, current events, readings, discussion, individual or small group activities, and opportunities to apply occupational health concepts to real problems.

Because this is a small in person section, some class meetings may function as seminar style discussions where students examine evidence, ask questions, discuss cases, and practice applying course concepts rather than relying exclusively on lecture.

Course materials are developed using Quarto to integrate scientific literature, data, maps, figures, Poll Everywhere activities, case studies, and other interactive teaching materials.

You are not expected to enter SHM 102 already knowing occupational health. You are expected to engage with the material, ask questions, practice applying new concepts, and communicate when you need help.

The course schedule remains flexible so that current events and useful occupational health cases can be incorporated as the quarter develops.

Readings and Preparatory Materials

Short readings, videos, current events, or other materials may occasionally be provided before class. Students are encouraged to review these materials before the corresponding class meeting. Doing so will provide more time to think about the material and prepare for discussion, but advance preparation is not required unless specifically stated otherwise.

When readings are used for an in class discussion or activity, time will be reserved during class for students to read or review the assigned material. Students who complete the reading beforehand may use this time to review the material, organize their thoughts, develop questions, or prepare for the discussion or activity.

Readings and preparatory materials are intended to support class activities, case discussions, and the development of occupational health reasoning rather than simply add additional work. Materials and instructions will be provided through Canvas.

SHM 102 is graded on a 400 point structure.

Final Project

The final project is worth 200 points and accounts for 50% of the total course grade.

The final project serves as the primary application based assessment in SHM 102.

Each student will individually investigate a real occupational or environmental health problem and apply the recurring course framework:

What's happening?
       ↓
Who is affected?
       ↓
What work or tasks are involved?
       ↓
What is the hazard?
       ↓
How are people exposed?
       ↓
What are the potential health effects?
       ↓
What factors influence risk?
       ↓
What does the evidence show?
       ↓
What controls would you recommend?
       ↓
How would you know whether the intervention worked?

Students will integrate credible evidence, occupational health concepts, exposure reasoning, prevention strategies, and professional communication into their analysis.

Project work will develop throughout the quarter. Final projects will be presented individually during the final week of instruction, November 30 through December 3.

Presentation time will be approximately 15 minutes per student, with approximately 10 to 12 minutes for the presentation and 3 to 5 minutes for questions and discussion. Peer review and audience participation during presentations contribute to Engagement & Current Events. Remaining presentation time may be used for approved rescheduling or presentation re-dos when appropriate.

No regularly scheduled SHM 102 coursework is planned during the final examination period, December 8 through December 11.

Engagement, Discussion, and Reflections

Preparation for class, participation in discussion, and engagement with course activities are important parts of learning in SHM 102. Engagement may include current event discussions, case studies, readings, Poll Everywhere questions, Canvas reflections, individual or small group activities, research exercises, peer review, and participation during final project presentations.

Engagement is based on meaningful participation rather than physical presence alone. Students are expected to listen, contribute constructively, ask questions, and engage respectfully with evidence and with one another. Students earn engagement credit by making a genuine effort to participate in and respond to assigned activities.

Short Canvas reflections are primarily completion based. Full credit is earned for a genuine, responsive attempt that addresses the prompt. A response does not need to reach a particular conclusion to earn credit. Blank, unrelated, or nonresponsive submissions receive no credit.

Attendance and Absences

Regular attendance is expected because many course activities depend on discussion, collaboration, and in class application. Attendance itself is not the sole basis for engagement points, but missing class may mean missing an activity that cannot be reproduced in exactly the same form.

If you know you will miss class, communicate as early as reasonably possible. If illness, emergency, university activity, religious accommodation, or another significant circumstance affects your ability to participate, contact Professor Rodriguez so that an appropriate plan can be discussed when possible.

Late Work, Make Up Work, and Extensions

Due dates are posted in Canvas. Students should communicate before a deadline whenever reasonably possible if circumstances will prevent timely completion. Extensions and make up opportunities are not automatic and depend on the nature of the activity and circumstances. Some live discussions, presentations, and collaborative activities cannot be recreated exactly after they occur.

Do not wait until the end of the quarter to raise a missing assignment or unresolved grading issue. Check Canvas regularly and communicate early.

Grades and Grade Questions

You are encouraged to ask how an assignment was evaluated, where points were lost, whether something may have been overlooked, or how to improve on future work. If you believe a grading error occurred, bring it to Professor Rodriguez’s attention promptly.

Grades are earned through the published course requirements. A grade will not be changed simply because a student needs a particular final grade, and individual extra credit opportunities will not be created for one student when they are unavailable to the rest of the class.

Communication and Professional Expectations

Occupational health professionals must be able to communicate scientific and technical information to workers, employers, communities, and other professionals. Throughout SHM 102, students will practice communicating occupational health information through discussion, short written reflections, case analysis, and the final project. Clear communication is more important than unnecessarily complicated language. Students should work toward explaining occupational health problems accurately and in a way that their intended audience can understand.

Use email or Canvas for short questions, clarification, absence notifications, and meeting requests. Complex questions about course performance, personal circumstances, project planning, or grades are usually better handled during office hours or an appointment.

When emailing, identify the course and make the question clear. A useful subject line is SHM 102 Question About [Topic or Assignment]. Students are responsible for checking Canvas announcements and CWU email regularly.

Inclement Weather and Transportation Disruptions

If weather or transportation conditions make an in person class impractical or unsafe, the class may move to a remote format or an asynchronous Canvas activity may replace that meeting. Any change will be announced through Canvas as early as reasonably possible. Students should not place themselves at unreasonable risk to travel to class and should communicate if local conditions affect their ability to participate.

Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence is not prohibited in SHM 102. Students are encouraged to use AI thoughtfully as a tool for learning, problem solving, brainstorming, clarifying concepts, and improving their understanding.

Students remain responsible for the accuracy, interpretation, reasoning, and recommendations contained in work they submit. AI should support rather than replace your own thinking. If AI materially contributes to submitted work, briefly identify the tool and explain where or how it was used. Verify factual claims and sources rather than assuming generated content is accurate.

Using AI to fabricate evidence or citations, misrepresent generated work as your own independent work, or bypass the learning purpose of an assignment may constitute academic dishonesty. If you are unsure whether a use is appropriate, ask before submitting the work.

Academic Integrity

Students are expected to act in accordance with CWU academic integrity and student conduct requirements. Submitted work must accurately represent your own contribution and must appropriately acknowledge the work, ideas, evidence, and tools used. Academic dishonesty may be addressed under CWU 501-20 and WAC 106-125-020.

Classroom Community and Conduct

CWU expects every member of the university community to contribute to an inclusive and respectful culture for all in its classrooms, work environments, and at campus events.

Occupational health involves real workers, communities, industries, injuries, illnesses, policies, and sometimes difficult events. We may disagree about interpretation or prevention strategies. Students should question ideas and evidence without attacking people. Asking someone to explain or support a position is part of academic inquiry.
