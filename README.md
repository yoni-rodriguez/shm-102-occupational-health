# SHM 102 — Occupational Health

**Central Washington University**

SHM 102 introduces students to the fundamental concepts of occupational health through a systems-based, case-study approach. The course examines how work, environmental conditions, and broader social and ecological systems influence worker health and how occupational health problems can be identified, assessed, and prevented.

---

## Course Organization

Rather than treating occupational hazards as isolated categories, the course examines worker health across multiple interconnected scales:

```text
Climate & Environment
       ↓
Society & Policy
       ↓
Community Health
       ↓
Workplace Environment
       ↓
Worker Health & Behaviors
       ↓
Cells, Molecules & Microorganisms
```

Chemical, biological, physical, ergonomic, and psychosocial hazards are examined across these scales through real-world occupational health cases.

Cases repeatedly use a common occupational health reasoning framework:

```text
Worker → Task → Hazard → Exposure → Health Effect → Risk → Controls
```

This framework provides students with a consistent approach for examining occupational health problems throughout the course.

---

## Course Approach

SHM 102 emphasizes:

- Real-world occupational and environmental health case studies
- Connections between global, national, state, and local worker health issues
- Scientific reasoning and evidence-based decision making
- Exposure pathways and health effects
- Prevention through the hierarchy of controls
- Interdisciplinary approaches to worker health
- Current occupational and environmental health events
- Active learning and application of course concepts

Course lectures are developed using Quarto to integrate scientific literature, data, maps, figures, interactive activities, and other teaching materials.

## Public Course Resource

This repository is intentionally maintained as a public educational resource.

Students may access course materials without needing to follow the instructor on GitHub and may continue to use the materials after completing SHM 102 or graduating from Central Washington University.

Public access also allows these materials to serve as an educational resource and example for students, instructors, and academic programs outside Central Washington University, subject to the copyright and licensing terms described below.
