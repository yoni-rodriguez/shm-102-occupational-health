# SHM 102 Occupational Health

**Central Washington University**

SHM 102 introduces students to the fundamental concepts of occupational health through a systems based, case study approach. The course examines how work, environmental conditions, and broader social and ecological systems influence worker health and how occupational health problems can be identified, assessed, and prevented.

## Course Organization

Rather than treating occupational hazards as isolated categories, the course examines worker health across multiple interconnected scales:

```text
Climate & Environment
       ↓
Society & Policy
       ↓
Community Health
       ↓
Workplace Environment
       ↓
Worker Health & Behaviors
       ↓
Cells, Molecules & Microorganisms
```

Biological, chemical, ergonomic, physical, and psychosocial hazards are examined across these scales through real world occupational health cases.

Cases repeatedly use a common occupational health reasoning framework:

```text
Worker → Task → Hazard → Exposure → Health Effect → Risk → Controls
```

This framework provides students with a consistent approach for examining occupational health problems throughout the course.

---

## Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

1. Explain occupational health professions and how they affect worker health.
2. Address significant occupational and environmental health issues and articulate their broader impacts.
3. Describe biological, chemical, ergonomic, physical, and psychosocial risk factors and hazards.
4. Assess the importance of prevention and proactive behavior in reducing risk factors and hazards.
5. Research occupational and environmental risk factors, hazards, exposures, and health effects for a selected work environment or problem.
6. Investigate how human factors can influence workplace health.
7. Prioritize recommendations using evidence and quantitative reasoning.
8. Apply the course reasoning framework: Worker → Task → Hazard → Exposure → Health Effect → Risk → Controls.

## Course Approach

SHM 102 emphasizes:

- Real world occupational and environmental health case studies
- Connections between global, national, state, and local worker health issues
- Scientific reasoning and evidence based decision making
- Exposure pathways and health effects
- Prevention through the hierarchy of controls
- Interdisciplinary approaches to worker health
- Current occupational and environmental health events
- Active learning and application of course concepts

The course begins with foundations and then increasingly uses cases, discussion, evidence, and application.

Class meetings may combine short lectures, case studies, current events, readings, discussion, individual or small group activities, and opportunities to apply occupational health concepts to real problems.

Some class meetings may function as seminar style discussions where students examine evidence, ask questions, discuss cases, and practice applying course concepts rather than relying exclusively on lecture.

Course materials are developed using Quarto to integrate scientific literature, data, maps, figures, Poll Everywhere activities, case studies, and other interactive teaching materials.

## Assessment Structure

SHM 102 uses a **400 point grading system**.

| Assessment | Points | Percent of Course Grade |
|---|---:|---:|
| Midterm: Foundations | 100 | 25% |
| Engagement & Current Events | 100 | 25% |
| Final Project | 200 | 50% |
| **Course Total** | **400** | **100%** |

The assessment structure is intentionally focused on understanding foundational concepts, engaging with occupational and environmental health problems throughout the quarter, and applying course concepts to a substantial real world case.

### Midterm: Foundations

The midterm is worth **100 points** and accounts for **25% of the total course grade**.

The midterm assesses foundational concepts and the ability to use them when reasoning through occupational health problems.

Students should be able to work with concepts such as:

- Public, environmental, and occupational health
- Hazard categories
- Hazard versus risk
- Exposure pathways and routes
- Dose, frequency, duration, and intensity
- Health effects
- Susceptibility
- Prevention
- Hierarchy of controls
- Occupational health reasoning

The emphasis is on using these concepts rather than memorizing definitions in isolation.

### Engagement & Current Events

Engagement & Current Events is worth **100 points** and accounts for **25% of the total course grade**.

Engagement may include:

- Current event discussions
- Case studies
- Readings
- Poll Everywhere activities
- Canvas reflections
- Individual or small group activities
- Research exercises
- Peer review
- Audience participation during final project presentations
- Other course application activities

Engagement emphasizes meaningful participation rather than physical presence alone.

### Final Project

The final project is worth **200 points** and accounts for **50% of the total course grade**.

The final project serves as the primary application based assessment in SHM 102.

Each student will individually investigate a real occupational or environmental health problem and apply the recurring course framework:

```text
What's happening?
       ↓
Who is affected?
       ↓
What work or tasks are involved?
       ↓
What is the hazard?
       ↓
How are people exposed?
       ↓
What are the potential health effects?
       ↓
What factors influence risk?
       ↓
What does the evidence show?
       ↓
What controls would you recommend?
       ↓
How would you know whether the intervention worked?
```

Students will integrate credible evidence, occupational health concepts, exposure reasoning, prevention strategies, and professional communication into their analysis.

Project work will develop throughout the quarter. Final projects will be presented individually during the final week of instruction, November 30 through December 3.

## Instructional Structure

SHM 102 meets four days per week in 50 minute class periods.

The course begins with foundational instruction in occupational and environmental health history, concepts, vocabulary, exposure, risk, and prevention.

After the foundations are established, instructional weeks may generally follow a recurring pattern:

```text
Monday
Introduce Problem / Case
        ↓
Tuesday
Build Scientific Concepts
        ↓
Wednesday
Apply / Investigate
        ↓
Thursday
Synthesis / Discussion / Project Work
```

This structure is intended to move students from encountering an occupational health problem to understanding the relevant science, applying evidence and course concepts, and reasoning toward prevention and control.

The weekly pattern is flexible. Current events, useful occupational health cases, class discussion, project development, and instructional pacing may change how an individual week is organized.

## Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence is not prohibited in SHM 102.

Students are encouraged to use AI thoughtfully as a tool for learning, problem solving, brainstorming, clarifying concepts, and improving their understanding.

Students remain responsible for the accuracy, interpretation, reasoning, evidence, and recommendations contained in work they submit.

AI should support rather than replace student thinking.

If AI materially contributes to submitted work, students should briefly identify the tool and explain where or how it was used. Factual claims and sources should be verified rather than assuming generated content is accurate.

Using AI to fabricate evidence or citations, misrepresent generated work as independent work, or bypass the learning purpose of an assignment may constitute academic dishonesty.

A useful principle for SHM 102 is:

> **Support the mechanics. Assess the reasoning.**
